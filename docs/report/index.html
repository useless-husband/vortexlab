vortexlab: validation report

A small from-scratch 2-D lattice-Boltzmann flow solver, checked against an exact solution and two published benchmarks, then used for one experiment on corner shapes of a square tower section.

這份報告由程式自動產生（make validate corners bench report）：先用三個有標準答案的題目檢查這個自己寫的流體解算器算得對不對，再拿它做一個實驗，比較方柱的角落切成不同形狀（直角、切角、圓角、內凹、台北 101 那種雙層內凹）時，阻力、升力擺動和渦流脫落頻率怎麼變。每個表格裡綠色勾代表數字落在論文公布的參考區間內，紅色叉代表落在外面，後面的百分比是超出多少。

Every number in the tables below was computed by the code in this repository on the machine described in section 5 and written to CSV files, from which this page is generated; nothing is typed in by hand. Reference values are in data/reference/*.csv with their sources, and the full citations are in docs/REFERENCES.md. A green check means the computed value lies inside the published reference interval, a red cross that it lies outside, followed by the distance to the nearer end of the interval.

1. Channel flow against the exact solution 管道流對照解析解

Flow between two parallel plates driven by a uniform force has the exact parabolic profile u(y) = g y (H − y) / 2ν. The channel is periodic along the flow, so the only error sources are the collision model and the wall rule. Resolution is doubled repeatedly with the relaxation time fixed (τ = 0.8) and the velocity scaled like 1/H.

Three things are checked: the error falls with the square of the resolution (observed order 2); with the two-relaxation-time model and Λ = 3/16 the halfway bounce-back wall is exactly where it should be and the parabola is reproduced to rounding error, as theory predicts (Ginzburg, Verhaeghe & d'Humières 2008); and with the walls placed a quarter or four fifths of a cell beyond the last fluid row, the interpolated bounce-back rule keeps second order. The last column is the drag on the two walls from the momentum-exchange force evaluation divided by the total driving force: it must be 1.

2. Lid-driven cavity against Ghia, Ghia & Shin (1982) 頂蓋驅動方腔

A square box of fluid whose lid slides at constant speed. Ghia, Ghia & Shin (1982) tabulated the velocity along the two centrelines at 17 points for several Reynolds numbers, computed with a second-order finite-difference multigrid method on a 129 × 129 grid; their tables have been the standard comparison ever since. Here: two-relaxation-time collision (Λ = 3/16), halfway bounce-back walls, lid speed 0.1 in lattice units (Mach 0.17), run until the largest velocity change over 1000 steps is below 10−9 of the lid speed.

Why the deviation from Ghia's table does not shrink with resolution. Ghia's numbers are themselves a second-order solution on a 129 × 129 grid. Compared with the later spectral benchmark of Botella & Peyret (1998), Ghia's tabulated extrema are off by up to 3.3 % at Re = 100 (the minimum of v) and 2.2 % at Re = 1000. The solver here converges towards the spectral values (second table below), and therefore away from Ghia's where the two differ; the largest deviation from Ghia sits at the same tabulated point at every resolution.

3. Cylinder in a channel: the Schäfer–Turek benchmark 圓柱繞流標準考題

The DFG benchmark of Schäfer & Turek (1996): a cylinder of diameter D = 0.1 m at (0.2, 0.2) m in a 2.2 m × 0.41 m channel with a parabolic inflow, ν = 10−3 m²/s, ρ = 1 kg/m³. The paper collects results submitted by a number of research groups; its tables end with intervals in which the authors estimated the exact values to lie. More precise values were published later by the same group and are shown as a second reference row.

Set-up here: uniform grid with the stated number of cells per diameter (the fluid region is 22n × 4.1n cells), interpolated bounce-back on the cylinder, velocity bounce-back inlet, constant-pressure outlet that absorbs plane sound waves, forces by momentum exchange, pressure difference from densities extrapolated quadratically to the front and rear points of the cylinder. Coefficients are 2F / (ρ U² D) with U the mean inflow velocity. The last rows of each table repeat runs at half the lattice velocity (half the Mach number, twice the number of steps) to show the size of the compressibility error.

Reading these tables. The 1996 intervals are narrow (±0.2 % for the steady drag, ±0.3 % for the maximum drag), and one of them does not contain the later reference value: the maximum lift of case 2D-2 from the later high-resolution computation is below the 1996 lower bound. Values that are outside an interval are marked as such, with the distance; they were not tuned.

Drag oscillation and Mach number. In case 2D-2 the peak-to-peak variation of the drag (maximum minus minimum) is about 0.051 at lattice velocity 0.05 at every resolution, against 0.063 in the later reference; at half the velocity it is 0.063-0.064. This is a compressibility effect, not a resolution effect: the channel is 22 diameters long and sound needs about as long to cross it as one drag oscillation lasts, so the pressure field cannot adjust along the whole channel at once as it does in an incompressible fluid. The 80-cell run was made at lattice velocity 0.05 only; from the half-velocity rows its maximum drag would be about 0.1 % higher, and its maximum lift up to 0.4 % lower, at half the Mach number. The maximum lift does not converge monotonically (0.4 % below the later reference at 40 cells, 0.8 % above at 80).

Run at 10 cells per diameter, case 2D-2 diverges (τ = 0.515 with velocity gradients the grid cannot represent); the program reports that instead of numbers, and the case is left out of the table.

4. Experiment: corner modifications of a square tower section 台北 101 的鋸齒角實驗

Taipei 101's plan is a square whose corners are stepped inwards twice. Its structural engineers report that in the wind-tunnel studies “a square tower with sharp corners creates large crosswind excitation. Rounded and chamfered (45°) corners reduced lateral response, but a ‘saw tooth’ or ‘double notch’ corner with 2.5 m notches achieved a dramatic reduction” (Poon, Shieh, Joseph & Chang 2004). Irwin (2008), of the wind-tunnel laboratory, writes that softening the corners “reduced the base wind-induced base bending moments by approximately 25%”. The crosswind excitation in question is vortex shedding: vortices peel off the two sides alternately and push the tower sideways at the shedding frequency.

台北 101 的平面是正方形、四個角各往內退兩階。結構設計者在論文裡說：風洞試驗顯示直角方塔的橫風向擺動很大，圓角和 45 度切角能減輕，而「鋸齒」（雙層內凹）角的效果最顯著；風洞實驗室的 Irwin 則寫道，修角讓基底彎矩降低約 25%。這裡用同一個解算器、同樣的雷諾數和阻塞比，比較五種角落形狀。

The experiment: five sections of the same width D in the same tunnel at the same Reynolds number. The corner size is b = 0.1 D for the chamfer (leg length), the rounding (radius) and the single recess (notch side); the double recess has two steps of 0.05 D each, so its total setback is also 0.1 D. (The published sources give the notch size of the real tower, 2.5 m, but not the plan width at the same level, so the real ratio is not taken from them; 0.1 D is the conventional size in the wind-engineering literature.) The stream is uniform, the side boundaries move with it and absorb sound, the section is at zero incidence. Each run starts from rest, the inflow is ramped up over 5 time units, the section is twisted briefly to break the symmetry, and statistics are taken over the second half of the run.

What the experiment shows, and what it cannot

Within this model (two-dimensional, laminar):

  • Every corner modification lowers the mean drag, by about 8-9 % at Re = 200 and 3.5-5 % at Re = 100, and raises the shedding frequency, by 12-15 % at Re = 200 and 3-5 % at Re = 100. Both say the same thing: the flow separates later or reattaches, and the wake is narrower. These two findings hold on both grids and at half the Mach number.
  • The lift fluctuation falls at Re = 200 (7-14 % on the main grid, rounded corners most), but this number is the least certain: the reduction is measured against the sharp square, whose own RMS lift changes by 5 % between the two grids, and on the coarser grid the reductions are 1-9 %. At Re = 100 the lift fluctuation is unchanged within ±3 %.
  • The four modifications are nearly interchangeable. The double recess is not better than a chamfer of the same size; its drag and Strouhal number are within 0.5 % of the chamfer's.

Checks on the numbers themselves: the modified sections change by less than 0.3 % in drag between 40 and 60 cells per side, the sharp square by 1.2 % in drag and 3 % in Strouhal number (sharp corners are the hard case for a uniform grid; Sohankar et al. report the same sensitivity). Halving the Mach number lowers drag by 1.0 % and RMS lift by 1.5-2.7 % but leaves the differences between sections essentially unchanged. Against Sohankar, Norberg & Davidson (1998) at the same 5 % blockage, the plain square here has 1.5 % more drag at both Reynolds numbers; the Strouhal number is 2 % higher at Re = 100 and 6 % higher at Re = 200, and the RMS lift is 9-20 % higher.

Relation to the real tower. Taipei 101 is three-dimensional, stands in a turbulent, sheared atmospheric wind, and has a Reynolds number of order 108; this simulation is two-dimensional and laminar at 100-200. What was measured for the tower is a structural response (base bending moment, acceleration), which depends on how well vortex shedding is correlated along the height and on how close the shedding frequency is to the building's natural frequency. None of that exists here.

Trends reproduced qualitatively: corner modification reduces drag and narrows the wake (reported for chamfered and rounded corners at high Reynolds number by Tamura & Miyagi 1999, as summarised by Dey & Das 2016), and the Strouhal number rises with corner rounding (Miran & Sohn 2015, at Re = 500).

Not reproduced: the designers' wind-tunnel observation that the double notch is far more effective than rounding or chamfering. In this model it is not. The mechanisms usually credited at full scale act on a turbulent separated shear layer and on the spanwise coherence of shedding; a 2-D laminar model contains neither, so this result says nothing against the wind-tunnel finding.

Not comparable: Irwin's 25 % reduction of the base bending moment. No quantity on this page is a base moment, and none of the percentages here should be set against it. No published 2-D laminar study of recessed corners was found to compare the recessed sections with.

小結：在這個二維層流模型裡，四種修角都讓平均阻力下降（Re = 200 時 8–9%）、渦流脫落頻率上升（12–15%），代表尾流變窄；升力擺動在 Re = 200 時下降 7–14%，但這個數字對網格最敏感；四種修角彼此幾乎沒有差別，雙層內凹並沒有比較好。這跟風洞裡「鋸齒角特別有效」的觀察不同，原因是真實情況是三維、紊流、雷諾數約一億，這個模型沒有那些機制，所以不能用來支持或反駁風洞的結論，更不能拿這裡的百分比去跟「基底彎矩降 25%」比。

Measured on an Apple M5 (10 cores, 16 GB) under macOS, shared with other jobs at the time, so the numbers vary by several per cent between runs. For scale: the lbmpy paper (Bauer, Köstler & Rüde 2021, section V.A) reports “about 300 MLUP/s” for a generated, AVX-512-vectorised D3Q19 single-relaxation-time kernel on all 20 cores of a Xeon Gold 6148 socket, limited by memory bandwidth; that is 15 MLUPS per core for a lattice with 19 populations per node, or about 285 million population updates per second per core. The scalar D2Q9 kernel here moves 9 populations per node. The comparison is between different lattices, collision models, machines and a decade of hardware; it shows only that the kernel is not unreasonably slow.

6. What this does not show 這份報告沒有證明的事

  • The solver is two-dimensional and laminar. Agreement with the benchmarks above says nothing about three-dimensional or turbulent flow.
  • The benchmarks cover Reynolds numbers 20 to 1000 in confined geometries. The corner experiment is run in an open tunnel configuration for which the only published comparison used here is the plain square.
  • Uniform grids only: the accuracy near the body is bought with cells everywhere.
  • Open boundaries are first-order; the compressibility error is of order Mach number squared (see the half-velocity rows).

限制：只有二維、層流；網格是均勻的；出口邊界只有一階精度；有馬赫數平方量級的可壓縮誤差。跟真實的台北 101（三維、雷諾數約一億、紊流）差距很大，第 4 節有說明哪些趨勢可以定性對照、哪些不行。
