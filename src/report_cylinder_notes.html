Reading these tables. The 1996 intervals are narrow (±0.2 % for the steady drag, ±0.3 % for the maximum drag), and one of them does not contain the later reference value: the maximum lift of case 2D-2 from the later high-resolution computation is below the 1996 lower bound. Values that are outside an interval are marked as such, with the distance; they were not tuned.

Drag oscillation and Mach number. In case 2D-2 the peak-to-peak variation of the drag (maximum minus minimum) is about 0.051 at lattice velocity 0.05 at every resolution, against 0.063 in the later reference; at half the velocity it is 0.063-0.064. This is a compressibility effect, not a resolution effect: the channel is 22 diameters long and sound needs about as long to cross it as one drag oscillation lasts, so the pressure field cannot adjust along the whole channel at once as it does in an incompressible fluid. The 80-cell run was made at lattice velocity 0.05 only; from the half-velocity rows its maximum drag would be about 0.1 % higher, and its maximum lift up to 0.4 % lower, at half the Mach number. The maximum lift does not converge monotonically (0.4 % below the later reference at 40 cells, 0.8 % above at 80).

Run at 10 cells per diameter, case 2D-2 diverges (τ = 0.515 with velocity gradients the grid cannot represent); the program reports that instead of numbers, and the case is left out of the table.
