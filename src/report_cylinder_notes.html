Reading these tables. The 1996 intervals are narrow (±0.2 % for the steady drag, ±0.3 % for the maximum drag), and one of them does not contain the later reference value: the maximum lift of case 2D-2 from the later high-resolution computation is below the 1996 lower bound. Values that are outside an interval are marked as such, with the distance; they were not tuned.

Drag oscillation and Mach number. In case 2D-2 the peak-to-peak variation of the drag (maximum minus minimum) is 0.050-0.053 at lattice velocity 0.05 on every grid, against 0.063 in the later reference. On a fixed grid, halving the velocity raises it by 21-28 % (to 0.063-0.066), while refining the grid four-fold at a fixed velocity changes it by 2-4 %: the deficit is a Mach-number effect, not a resolution effect. It is not a plain O(Ma²) error, though: halving the velocity once more (U = 0.0125, Ma 0.022) lowers the amplitude again by 3-5 %, to 3 % below the reference at 20 and 40 cells, where an O(Ma²) error would keep its sign and shrink four-fold. The table above lists the ratios of the successive changes for each quantity; only the Strouhal number behaves like an O(Ma²) error. A possible reading, not tested here: sound needs 1.9, 0.95 and 0.48 convective time units to travel the 22-diameter channel at the three velocities, the drag oscillates with a period of 1.66 units, and the amplitude may follow how these two times compare. At 80 cells, halving the velocity moved the maximum drag by only +0.015 % and lowered the maximum lift by 0.45 %; all four benchmark quantities stay inside the 1996 intervals. The maximum lift does not converge monotonically with the grid (0.4-1.0 % below the later reference at 40 cells, 0.4-0.8 % above at 80).

Run at 10 cells per diameter, case 2D-2 diverges (τ = 0.515 with velocity gradients the grid cannot represent); the program reports that instead of numbers, and the case is left out of the table.
