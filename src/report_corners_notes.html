What the experiment shows, and what it cannot

Within this model (two-dimensional, laminar):

  • Every corner modification lowers the mean drag, by about 8-9 % at Re = 200 and 3.5-5 % at Re = 100, and raises the shedding frequency, by 12-15 % at Re = 200 and 3-5 % at Re = 100. Both say the same thing: the flow separates later or reattaches, and the wake is narrower. These two findings hold on both grids and at half the Mach number.
  • The lift fluctuation falls at Re = 200 (7-14 % on the main grid, rounded corners most), but this number is the least certain: the reduction is measured against the sharp square, whose own RMS lift changes by 5 % between the two grids, and on the coarser grid the reductions are 1-9 %. At Re = 100 the lift fluctuation is unchanged within ±3 %.
  • The four modifications are nearly interchangeable. The double recess is not better than a chamfer of the same size; its drag and Strouhal number are within 0.5 % of the chamfer's.

Checks on the numbers themselves: the modified sections change by less than 0.3 % in drag between 40 and 60 cells per side, the sharp square by 1.2 % in drag and 3 % in Strouhal number (sharp corners are the hard case for a uniform grid; Sohankar et al. report the same sensitivity). Halving the Mach number lowers drag by 1.0 % and RMS lift by 1.5-2.7 % but leaves the differences between sections essentially unchanged. Against Sohankar, Norberg & Davidson (1998) at the same 5 % blockage, the plain square here has 1.5 % more drag at both Reynolds numbers; the Strouhal number is 2 % higher at Re = 100 and 6 % higher at Re = 200, and the RMS lift is 9-20 % higher.

Relation to the real tower. Taipei 101 is three-dimensional, stands in a turbulent, sheared atmospheric wind, and has a Reynolds number of order 108; this simulation is two-dimensional and laminar at 100-200. What was measured for the tower is a structural response (base bending moment, acceleration), which depends on how well vortex shedding is correlated along the height and on how close the shedding frequency is to the building's natural frequency. None of that exists here.

Trends reproduced qualitatively: corner modification reduces drag and narrows the wake (reported for chamfered and rounded corners at high Reynolds number by Tamura & Miyagi 1999, as summarised by Dey & Das 2016), and the Strouhal number rises with corner rounding (Miran & Sohn 2015, at Re = 500).

Not reproduced: the designers' wind-tunnel observation that the double notch is far more effective than rounding or chamfering. In this model it is not. The mechanisms usually credited at full scale act on a turbulent separated shear layer and on the spanwise coherence of shedding; a 2-D laminar model contains neither, so this result says nothing against the wind-tunnel finding.

Not comparable: Irwin's 25 % reduction of the base bending moment. No quantity on this page is a base moment, and none of the percentages here should be set against it. No published 2-D laminar study of recessed corners was found to compare the recessed sections with.

小結：在這個二維層流模型裡，四種修角都讓平均阻力下降（Re = 200 時 8–9%）、渦流脫落頻率上升（12–15%），代表尾流變窄；升力擺動在 Re = 200 時下降 7–14%，但這個數字對網格最敏感；四種修角彼此幾乎沒有差別，雙層內凹並沒有比較好。這跟風洞裡「鋸齒角特別有效」的觀察不同，原因是真實情況是三維、紊流、雷諾數約一億，這個模型沒有那些機制，所以不能用來支持或反駁風洞的結論，更不能拿這裡的百分比去跟「基底彎矩降 25%」比。
