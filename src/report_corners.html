Taipei 101's plan is a square whose corners are stepped inwards twice. Its structural engineers report that in the wind-tunnel studies “a square tower with sharp corners creates large crosswind excitation. Rounded and chamfered (45°) corners reduced lateral response, but a ‘saw tooth’ or ‘double notch’ corner with 2.5 m notches achieved a dramatic reduction” (Poon, Shieh, Joseph & Chang 2004). Irwin (2008), of the wind-tunnel laboratory, writes that softening the corners “reduced the base wind-induced base bending moments by approximately 25%”. The crosswind excitation in question is vortex shedding: vortices peel off the two sides alternately and push the tower sideways at the shedding frequency.

台北 101 的平面是正方形、四個角各往內退兩階。結構設計者在論文裡說：風洞試驗顯示直角方塔的橫風向擺動很大，圓角和 45 度切角能減輕，而「鋸齒」（雙層內凹）角的效果最顯著；風洞實驗室的 Irwin 則寫道，修角讓基底彎矩降低約 25%。這裡用同一個解算器、同樣的雷諾數和阻塞比，比較五種角落形狀。

The experiment: five sections of the same width D in the same tunnel at the same Reynolds number. The corner size is b = 0.1 D for the chamfer (leg length), the rounding (radius) and the single recess (notch side); the double recess has two steps of 0.05 D each, so its total setback is also 0.1 D. (The published sources give the notch size of the real tower, 2.5 m, but not the plan width at the same level, so the real ratio is not taken from them; 0.1 D is the conventional size in the wind-engineering literature.) The stream is uniform, the side boundaries move with it and absorb sound, the section is at zero incidence. Each run starts from rest, the inflow is ramped up over 5 time units, the section is twisted briefly to break the symmetry, and statistics are taken over the second half of the run.
